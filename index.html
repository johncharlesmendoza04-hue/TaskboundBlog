What is Klint Maximal?

Klint Maximal is the flagship model made by Taskbound. Its base model is GPT-6 Luna, but more improved and optimized. Each request is assembled from a long builder prompt, an effort note and, at High and Very high effort, a private plan written by a first model call. The answer is then generated with the effort level passed to the API.

"Maximal" is the top effort setting of this model: the most planning, the deepest reasoning and a double quality pass. Because its base is GPT-6 Luna, we price it at Luna's published per-token rates, so the figures below are an estimate of the Klint Maximal bill, not an official Taskbound price list.

How we ran the numbers

We assume one typical build: an 8,000-token prompt (system instructions plus history) and a complete page of about 12,000 to 14,000 output tokens. Reasoning tokens are billed as output. For each effort level, these are the totals across all calls in the request:

The cost results

At Maximal effort a build on Klint Maximal costs under two cents, and the same token profile on Astra costs about a hundred times more. The effort ladder is a smaller lever than the model you pick: going from Very low to Very high multiplies the bill on any one model by roughly five. Thinking tokens are about half of Maximal's output (16k of 32.5k in our profile), so that's the part to watch.

Klint Maximal vs famous models

Klint Maximal is priced at the rate of its base model, GPT-6 Luna, which OpenRouter describes as the fast, cost-efficient model below GPT-6 Sol, with a 1.05M-token context. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash has open weights, and its price comes from Hugging Face. The GPT models (Luna, Sol and Astra) are priced from OpenRouter, and the Gemini models come from Klint's built-in model list. Sol's $4/$20 rate was a promotion through at least November 21, 2026. Gemini 4 Argon is shown at its post-promotion $4/$20 rate, which Klint's built-in list gives as the price after November 30. Claude models are left out because we didn't verify current prices for this post.

Performance: what we can say

In our hands-on use, the quality of Klint Maximal's results is at about the level of Claude Sonnet 3.5 Medium. That is an overall impression from using it, not a measured result: we haven't run a formal benchmark on Klint Maximal, so we make no score claims. Public benchmarks exist for the other models, but they test other tasks and say little about generating a polished web page.

What the design does suggest is narrower. Taskbound has improved and optimized the Luna base for page building. A planning pass catches layout and logic mistakes before code is written. A double quality pass at Very high effort should reduce visible defects. Patch-style edits rewrite only changed parts, which cuts output tokens on follow-up requests. All three trade time and money for fewer retries. Whether they win that trade is something only testing can tell us.

A test you can run yourself

Run 20 fixed prompts across web, game, dashboard and app modes, three runs each, at every effort level on Klint Maximal and, for comparison, on each rival model. Record tokens, wall-clock time, whether the output runs without console errors, and a blind pairwise preference score from two reviewers. Report cost per accepted build, not cost per call.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Built on GPT-6 Luna and further improved and optimized by Taskbound.
  • Very cheap: under two cents per Maximal build in our profile, the lowest of the six.
  • Result quality is at about the level of Claude Sonnet 3.5 Medium, in our hands-on use.
  • Effort is adjustable per request, so simple questions need not pay for deep thinking.
  • Planning and quality passes target the common failure of half-finished or untested code.
  • Patch edits and interactive answer blocks reduce wasted output.

Cons

  • Its quality gain over plain GPT-6 Luna is unmeasured.
  • Very high effort is slow and roughly five times costlier than Very low.
  • Reasoning shares the output budget, so a long think can leave no room for the answer.
  • The long builder prompt adds input tokens to every call.
  • Some providers do not stream reasoning, so the thinking panel can stay empty.

The takeaway

Klint Maximal is the cheapest model in this comparison at its top setting, at about two cents per build, against $1.79 for GPT-6 Astra. That's cheap enough to leave on by default and judge by results. Our own impression is that quality lands at about the level of Claude Sonnet 3.5 Medium, but that is not yet a measured result, so the sensible next step is the test above, run on Klint Maximal and one or two rivals.

Where the prices came from

  • OpenRouter: GPT-6 Luna, GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-6 Astra pricing, plus Luna's context size. Luna's rate is what we use for Klint Maximal.
  • Hugging Face: DeepSeek V4.1 Flash pricing.
  • Built in: Gemini 3.8 Flash and Gemini 4 Argon prices, from Klint's built-in model list.

List prices change; recheck before budgeting.
